The lifecycle view shows the stable semantic progression that governs how an artifact moves from initial observation to a
defended trust decision.

        A                          B                          C                           D                            E             F

      Reconnaissance              Fingerprinting

        observe source            establish identity         inspect containers          materialize safely                analyze   govern trust




                                       Artifact state changes at every phase - not merely task completion.
                                Identity, structure, materialization, findings, evidence and trust are progressively constrained.




    WHAT THIS DIAGRAM REPRESENTS

    This diagram shows the stable six-stage semantic path an artifact follows from initial observation to a governed trust
    decision. Each transition represents a change in what ATLAS knows about the artifact or is authorized to do with it,
    rather than a generic dependency between tasks. Structural Discovery deliberately precedes Controlled Extraction so
    container risk can be evaluated before potentially hostile content is materialized on disk. Deep Understanding remains
    an extensible analysis boundary where tools, parsers, security engines, or models can produce normalized findings
    without inheriting authority over the lifecycle. Review / Promotion is the final trust gate, ensuring that analyzer output
    does not automatically become authoritative downstream data.




        DESIGN IMPLICATION

        Preserve this A-F sequence as the semantic spine even if future implementations add workers, queues, APIs, or internal DAG
        behavior.
